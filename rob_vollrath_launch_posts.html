The CategoryFlag in the ground — who Rob is
112 WORDS

An emergency responder station is not an office building.

These are buildings that run 24 hours a day and never close. The people inside sleep there, eat there, and breathe whatever the mechanical systems are doing to the air for a third of their lives.

One of the five Cleveland police stations we just finished broke ground in 1973. Three of the five had HVAC that had stopped working entirely.

You can defer a lot of things in a building. The air in a room where first responders sleep between calls is not one of them.

That is the work I care about. Not the square footage — who is inside it.

Note: Voice: flat opener, specifics do the work, ends on substance not a slogan. Every figure here is public record.

The ProofCleveland, with the disciplined number
134 WORDS

Cleveland asked us to overhaul five police stations. Here is what that meant.

Five district stations. A $21 million investment, procured under Ohio Revised Code 717.02 — which let the city do the work without raising taxes or issuing a bond. The savings from the improvements pay for the improvements.

At the kickoff, we put our company-average number on the public record: across our projects, we deliver about 120% of the savings we forecast.

On Cleveland specifically, the early numbers are running higher than that. Materially higher.

I am not going to publish the figure until our engineers finish confirming it. When they do, I will show you the math — not the headline, the math.

That is the difference between a projection and a guarantee. We put our name on the second one.

The StakesWhy it matters — understated
110 WORDS

Three of the five Cleveland police stations had HVAC that no longer worked.

Not underperforming. Not inefficient. Not working.

These are buildings staffed around the clock by the people we call on the worst day of our lives — and the systems meant to keep the air breathable had simply stopped, in some cases years earlier.

Nobody decided to let that happen. It happened the way deferred maintenance always happens in a public building: quietly, one budget cycle at a time, until “we’ll fix it next year” has been said fifteen times.

The savings pay for the work. That is the mechanism.

Who is in the building is the reason.

Note: Restraint is what makes this land. No sentimentality — the repetition ('not working, not working') does the emotional work, then Rob steps back to plain fact. Do not add adjectives.

The Fire StationsForward flag — mission, not pipeline
123 WORDS

At the Cleveland police station kickoff, a councilman said something on the record that most of the room moved right past.

He said we would move on to the firehouses next.

I did not move past it.

Fire stations are, if anything, the harder case. Older building stock. The same round-the-clock occupancy. Air-quality and equipment demands a typical municipal building never has to consider. And the same budget math that leaves a station running on failed HVAC for years.

We have now proven the model on five emergency responder buildings in one city — under a savings guarantee, at no new cost to taxpayers.

The firehouses were promised on the public record. I intend to be the one who pays that promise off.
