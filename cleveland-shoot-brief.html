02 / The Split

Match Shots

Split-screens will do 80% of the persuasion in the final cut. That makes match shots the highest-value pixels of the day. Nail these first — before interviews, before beauty B-roll, before anything else.

How to Match

The Technique

  1. Load the "before" reference images on a phone or tablet. Bring both to every site.
  2. At the shot, hold the reference next to the viewfinder. Match camera position, height, and angle by eye.
  3. Shoot slightly wider than the reference — gives us room to reframe in edit.
  4. Locked-off static first. That's the split-screen master.
  5. If time, a second take with a slow push — for the beauty cut.
  6. Match the time of day if you can. Don't shoot golden hour if the reference is high noon.

Storyboard below is illustrative. The actual references will drive framing on the day.

04 / The Route

The Five Stations

Priority isn't equal. Sequence the day around transformation drama — which station changed the most is the flagship. Spend the most time there. It's the opener and closer of the final video.

  1. Flagship station (the one that changed the most): 90+ minutes. All match shots. Primary interview backdrop.
  2. Two secondary stations: 30-45 minutes each. Match shots only. Interviews if scheduling flexes.
  3. Remaining stations: 15-20 minutes each. Exterior match shot only. Skip if the day tightens.

Gio and Justin will know which is which. Trust their read on where the transformation is most photogenic.
