GATOR — Brand & Operational Infrastructure

Prepared for GATOR Leadership May 2026

GATOR is closing faster than the team can fulfill. Two sales reps closing six to eight cities per month means six to eight implementations needing brand consistency, onboarding infrastructure, RFP response templates, and customer-facing content. Without operational scaffolding, the closes don't survive year two. This document outlines two structures for building that scaffolding alongside the sales engine.

00Two Paths Forward

Both paths are valid. Both deliver the operational scaffolding GATOR needs. The difference is structure, commitment, and cost predictability.

PATH A Salary + Equity (Inside the Company)
  • Brandon as Chief Brand Officer, fractional (25–30 hrs/week)
  • $110K salary + $3K/month VDO retainer for ongoing technical support
  • 1.5% common stock with structured vesting and acceleration
  • VDO Aigency parks new client acquisition to focus on GATOR
  • Brand and operational infrastructure built in-house, aligned with equity
  • Project production work billed separately at market rates as scope arises
PATH B Vendor Engagement (Outside the Company)
  • VDO Aigency engaged as an outside vendor at market rates
  • Production work, case studies, onboarding videos, brand deliverables billed per project
  • No salary commitment, no equity grant
  • VDO continues operating its independent client base
  • Brandon available as an informal advisor on brand and content strategy
  • Lower monthly burn; brand and operations function remains external to the company
Which Serves GATOR

Path A makes sense if GATOR is scaling toward Series A and benefits from a committed core team on the cap table when investors begin diligence. Path B makes sense if the current stage favors lower fixed costs and an external production partner. The rest of this document details the scope, deliverables, and compensation structure of Path A. Path B requires no further documentation — it is simply ongoing project work between GATOR and VDO Aigency at standard rates.

01The Operational Gap

Closing customers is half the equation. The other half is keeping them.

Municipal procurement is a relationship business. Cities talk to each other. One mismanaged rollout in Florida kills three pipeline closes in Georgia. One inconsistent brand presence across podcasts, decks, and conferences signals to procurement directors that GATOR isn't built for scale.

Sales teams generate revenue. Operations and brand infrastructure protect it. The cost of not building this scaffolding is higher than the cost of building it.

What scales when sales scales

  • Customer onboarding documentation (one per city, replicable)
  • Case study production (real footage, real testimonials, distributed to next prospects)
  • RFP response templates (sales team needs polished material on day one of every new pursuit)
  • Sales enablement assets (decks, one-pagers, demo videos)
  • Brand consistency across leadership in every public moment
  • Internal workflow automation

02Scope of Role — Chief Brand Officer

Fractional role. Approximately 25–30 hours per week. Reports to CEO. Coordinates with finance on reporting cadence and with sales on enablement requirements.

First 90 days — concrete deliverables

PHASE 01 Brand & Identity
  • GATOR visual identity system (logo, color, typography, document templates)
  • Brand voice guide (tone, vocabulary, what GATOR sounds like in every channel)
  • Web presence baseline (landing page, sales-facing materials)
PHASE 02 Sales Enablement
  • Master sales deck for the sales team
  • RFP response template (reusable across procurement processes)
  • One-pagers per use case (supplies aggregation, health procurement, fleet, IT)
  • Demo video assets (product walk-through, customer outcomes)
PHASE 03 Customer Success Infrastructure
  • Onboarding playbook (replicable per city, used by every new account)
  • Training materials for municipal staff (video and document)
  • Case study production with closed customers (Terre Haute, Miami-Dade, Doral, Evansville)
PHASE 04 Public Presence
  • Executive podcast and conference preparation (talking points, wardrobe, message consistency)
  • Social channel strategy for leadership team
  • Content calendar for thought leadership and customer stories
PHASE 05 Internal Workflow Automation
  • Automation of redundant manual tasks identified during early implementation
  • Internal tooling for sales handoff, customer onboarding, and reporting
  • Light integration scaffolding between internal systems

03What's Not in Scope

Clear boundaries protect the budget and the working relationships.

  • Sales. Sales hires report through the sales function, not through this role.
  • Operations and financial reporting. Handled by the finance function.
  • Core product engineering. The GATOR platform itself — process automation engines, web applications for sales operations, the procurement aggregation and reverse auction infrastructure. These are separate engineering conversations as the product team scales.
  • Project-based production work beyond the 90-day deliverables. Additional case studies, demo films, event production, or marketing campaigns are billed as separate projects through VDO Aigency at market rates.

04Compensation Structure

The total cash outlay is structured to keep monthly burn predictable and to ensure VDO's continuous availability for technical support.

Base Salary $110,000 / year
Brandon's time on brand strategy, voice, content direction, executive presence, and team coordination.
Retainer to VDO $3,000 / month
Covers continuous availability for internal workflow automation and tooling (approximately 25 hours per week of technical capacity). Paid to VDO Aigency, which manages execution.
Production Projects Billed separately at market rates
Case study videos, demo films, RFP visuals, event production, and any project work beyond the 90-day deliverables. Billed transparently as scope arises. Per-project approval.
Equity 1.5% common stock
2-year vest. 6-month cliff. 50% acceleration on Series A. 100% acceleration on acquisition. Pro-rata rights on all future rounds.
LLC Contingency Equity grant documented and signed at LLC formation. Vesting clock starts on signed letter of intent (not LLC formation date).
Note on Budget

The $3K monthly retainer covers internal workflow automation, not the full "marketing engine." Production work scales transparently as needed. This means the monthly burn is predictable; project work is approved scope-by-scope. No surprise expenses, no scope creep.

05Why the Equity Structure Matters

Five cities — Terre Haute, Miami-Dade, South Miami, Evansville, and Doral — closed before entity formation. A senior sales hire from a major ERP competitor. The Series A conversation is closer than 12 months out.

Locking equity terms before Series A protects both sides:

  1. Investors see a committed core team in writing. Vested or vesting key operators with skin in the game makes the cap table cleaner during diligence.
  2. Brandon doesn't park VDO Aigency on a handshake. Without written terms, parking an existing business for 18–24 months is a non-starter.
  3. GATOR gets retention insurance. The 50% Series A acceleration plus continued vesting means Brandon is incentivized to stay through scaling, not exit at the first liquidity event.
  4. Acquisition acceleration (100%) protects against rapid exit scenarios. Strategic acquirers — Tyler, Oracle, larger ERP players — close deals fast. The clause ensures core operators don't lose unvested equity in a sub-24-month sale.
