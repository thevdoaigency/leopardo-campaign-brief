SPEC.05 · VOICE
Quiet · confident.
DO/DON'T LIVE COMPARISON
▰ DEPLOY
"Five cities. Closed before the entity was formed."
Specific. Numerical. Past-tense confident. The fact does the persuasion. A CMO can quote this verbatim in a board meeting.
▰ REJECT
"Innovative AI-driven procurement solutions!"
No category language. No exclamation. Procurement directors buy boring competence, not innovation. Exclamation marks read as desperation.
▰ DEPLOY
"$418,000 saved in year one. Terre Haute, Indiana."
Real city, real number, real outcome. Receipts before adjectives. The mayor's office can verify every word.
▰ REJECT
"Significant savings for forward-thinking cities."
No vague magnitudes. No "forward-thinking." Cities don't want to be flattered; they want documented results.
▰ DEPLOY
"Built on decades of municipal experience."
The Leopardo lineage stated plainly. One short sentence carries the entire credibility argument.
▰ REJECT
"Leveraging deep industry expertise to unlock value."
No leveraging. No unlocking. No "value." Real verbs, real outcomes, fewer syllables. Strip every word that does no work.

SPEC.07 · PRINCIPLES
Three rules. Non-negotiable.
ENFORCED ACROSS ALL DELIVERABLES
i.
Family first.
Every GATOR asset carries the endorsement line "A Leopardo Technology." Same fonts. Same blue. The parent relationship is structural, not decorative.
ii.
Real cities. Real numbers.
Terre Haute, not "midwest cities." $418,000, not "significant savings." 27%, not "double-digit improvements." Never reach for the generic when the specific is available.
iii.
Quiet over loud.
No exclamation. No revolution. No category language. Procurement directors don't buy from loud rooms. The work persuades; the brand doesn't have to.
